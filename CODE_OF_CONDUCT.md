# Code of Conduct

MITRA is a place to learn in public, which means being visibly bad at things for a while.
That only works if everyone here is decent about it.

## Expected

- **Assume good faith.** A "stupid question" is someone trusting you enough to ask.
- **Critique the code, not the person.** "This will break on empty input" is useful.
  "This is garbage" is not.
- **Help when you can.** Answering someone's doubt is the fastest way to find out whether
  you actually understood it.
- **Do your own work.** Learn from anything — tutorials, AI, each other. Don't submit
  someone else's work as yours.
- **Credit your sources.** If you followed a tutorial or used generated code, say so in
  your README. Nobody loses marks for honesty.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, discrimination, or personal attacks of any kind
- Sexual or violent content, or unwelcome attention
- Publishing someone's private information
- Plagiarising another student's submission
- Deliberately breaking the repo, CI, or someone else's branch
- Sustained disruption of discussions or reviews

## Scope

This applies to everything in and around the program: issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, commit messages, and any MITRA chat or call.

## Reporting

Report a problem to a mentor privately — tag `@RishiR123` in a private channel, or email
the program address given in your onboarding. Reports are handled confidentially.

Consequences escalate as needed: a private word, a public warning, removal from a thread,
removal of write access, or removal from the program.

Mentors are held to this standard too. If a mentor is the problem, say so — to another
mentor or the program lead.
